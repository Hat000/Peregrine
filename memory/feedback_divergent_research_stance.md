Fengyou, 2026-06-27 (VQ2 stack-design research phase): the commander was "jumpy" — declared the champion-racer "spine settled and validated" ~1 hour into a **2-day** research window. Corrected.

**Why:** (1) Premature convergence throws away the exploration that produces the win. Everything is subject to change until the window closes. (2) **Competitive-edge logic:** if WE found the champion spine (CTBR + gate-progress reward + gate-relative obs + perception-aware look-at + privileged critic) this easily from public papers, so did every serious competitor — matching it yields ZERO planning edge. The advantage is in the **novel, not-yet-documented** solutions and the angles specific to OUR setup that prior art does not exploit (per-load track randomization, multi-lap-within-a-load recon+exploit, photorealism, a full 8 GB onboard GPU, no real-world sim-to-real hardware gap). The more effort spent here, the more likely we place well. (3) Future force-multiplier: Fengyou + frontier models (Gemini, GPT-5.6) can invent solutions to as-yet-unsolved problems — the research phase exists to set that up.

**How to apply:** In design/research phases — (a) treat the consensus/"spine" as the *contested baseline to beat*, never the answer; (b) keep EVERY fork alive until it is provably terrible, kill only the truly disadvantageous; (c) when digesting prior art, extract where it is **weak / beatable / admits open problems**, not just what it does — that's where novel work lives; (d) explicitly seek the asymmetric edge unique to our parameters; (e) do not say "settled/validated/decided" until convergence is actually warranted by the timeline and evidence; (f) 🚩 **a confident PREDICTION (even 90–95% from a frontier model) does NOT close a fork — only a cheap TEST that actually fails closes it. Fengyou's standing rule: we have historically closed doors too viciously and missed improvements.** When a reviewer is highly confident a fork fails, that is a reason to find the CHEAPEST falsifying test and keep the door open until it runs — not to slam it. Cheap tests are how you keep many doors open without paying rent. Also: read confident "it fails" verdicts for where they OVERSTATE *our* specific case (e.g. a critique of dense-map gate geometry doesn't kill a corridor-only use), and for the door the diagnosis *opens* (a reachability finding redirects the fix, it doesn't end the work).
